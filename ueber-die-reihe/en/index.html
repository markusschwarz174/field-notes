About this series

Written by me — but no longer alone.

Instead of writing about the who and the what, I realize that I am at the beginning of a time when the with-what will become a matter of course. But I still wonder who or what this with-what is: a tool, a counterpart — or both?

01 · About this series

The series ‹Field Notes — AI and Education› is the serialized development diary of a teacher. It follows my journey with AI from July 2025 to around January 2026. It documents and reflects on my own use of AI and its effects on thinking and on everyday classroom practice. The individual Field Notes combine personal observations, concrete working steps, and retrospective reflections. This page will continue to be maintained and may therefore differ from archived versions at a later date.

02 · How this series came about

This series grew out of an attempt to use artificial intelligence for lesson preparation and covers the period from July 2025 to January 2026. At first, I did not intend to document this experiment. The Field Notes themselves were not written until April 2026. The working material used for the reconstruction comprises around 600 indexed documents totaling about 5,600 pages from the period between July 2025 and January 2026. It includes real chat histories, authentic prompts, data and processes, teaching materials, planning documents, interim versions, and other working artifacts. The reproduced chat excerpts were deliberately retained with their language errors, inaccuracies, and wording from the time.

This material had to be placed into a coherent context afterward. Especially between August and November 2025, many developments were taking place at the same time and not always ‹according to plan›. To make the account understandable, I therefore had to select and condense the material; developments that had occurred in parallel were arranged into a coherent chronological sequence.

The Field Notes were not written by an AI.
I wrote them — but not without AI.

They were created from April 2026 onward in a multi-month, iterative process in which human and AI continually developed their collaboration and their roles in the shared writing process. Among other things, the AI took on the following roles:

⮱Tool—helped organize extensive chat histories, develop code, and manage technical steps ranging from design to publication.
⮱Sparring partner—reviewed drafts, pushed back, developed alternatives, and helped sharpen decisions.
⮱Thinking mirror—made patterns, connections, and blind spots visible and allowed me to look back on my own development process.

These shifting roles do not make the question of ‹with AI› or ‹without AI› wrong — but it can no longer be answered unequivocally. I therefore see no future in drawing this boundary. Instead, I face the broader question: How can I take responsibility for my thinking and writing in interaction with AI in the future?

The documentation of the Field Notes series begins in January 2026 with the review and organization of the available sources, followed by the writing of the Notes from April 2026 onward — and ends when the final Note is uploaded on [Datum noch offen].

I document how this project was created and how I worked on it in detail in a separate Field Note. In it, I also examine how the shared writing process developed and what I learned from it for my collaboration with AI on extensive texts.

03 · To avoid any misunderstanding

I write here in an essayistic form, from my own practice and perspective — not as a peer-reviewed academic publication. The observations and assessments presented are personal and have not been empirically validated.

And photographs of student work were deconstructed to protect the people involved. Names and other identifying details were anonymized.

Without me, what is written here would not be here; and I am responsible for which decisions in it are mine and which traces belong to AI.
