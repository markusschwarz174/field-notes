## Written by me — but no longer alone

Instead of writing about the who and the what, I realize: I am at the beginning of a time when the with-what is becoming a matter of course for me. But what or who is this with-what? Is it a tool, a counterpart, or something else?

## 01 · About this series

The series ‹Field Notes — AI and Education› is my serialized development diary as a teacher. It documents and reflects on my use of AI and its effects on my thinking and actions in everyday teaching. The individual Field Notes combine personal observations, concrete working steps, and retrospective reflections.

## 02 · How this series came about

It began with an experiment: I wanted to use artificial intelligence to prepare my lessons. Between July 2025 and January 2026, this developed into a journey that I had not initially intended to document. In January 2026, I began reviewing and organizing the available sources. In April 2026, I started turning them into the Field Notes.

The working material used for the reconstruction comprises around 600 indexed documents totaling about 5,600 pages. It includes real chat histories, authentic prompts, data and processes, teaching materials, planning documents, interim versions, and other working artifacts. I deliberately retained the reproduced chat excerpts with their language errors, inaccuracies, and wording from the time.

I had to place all this material into a coherent context afterward. Especially between August and November 2025, many developments were taking place at the same time and not always ‹according to plan›. To make the account understandable, I therefore selected and condensed the material; developments that had occurred in parallel were arranged into a coherent chronological sequence.

**The Field Notes were not written automatically by an AI.<br>
I wrote them — but not without AI.**

They emerged through a multi-month, iterative process in which the AI and I continually developed our collaboration while writing. Among other things, the AI took on the following roles:

- **Tool** — helped organize extensive chat histories, develop code, and manage technical steps ranging from design to publication.
- **Sparring partner** — reviewed drafts, pushed back, developed alternatives, and helped sharpen decisions.
- **Thinking mirror** — made patterns, connections, and blind spots visible and allowed me to look back on my own development process.

These shifting roles raise the question of whom the intellectual work in the shared writing process can be attributed to. Thoughts, objections, and formulations emerged through exchange; they were taken up, discarded, and developed further. The distinction between ‹made by AI› and ‹made by a human› is not fundamentally wrong. With short, clearly defined tasks, the traces can often be attributed unambiguously. But the longer I work with the AI on a demanding question, the more our steps of reasoning begin to interlock. My thoughts change the AI’s answers, and its answers change the further course of my thinking. This creates a kind of spiral of thought in which each new turn builds on the previous one. The traces blur not through carelessness, but as a consequence of the collaboration. Instead of trying to determine their origin retrospectively, I therefore ask myself a broader question: How can I further develop my thinking and writing in collaboration with AI and, above all, take responsibility for it?

I describe how this series was created and how I worked on it in detail in a separate Field Note. In it, I also examine how the shared writing process developed over the months and what I learned from it for my collaboration with AI on extensive texts. This Field Note is my answer to the question: ‹And what role did I take on?›

## 03 · To avoid any misunderstanding

I write here in essayistic form, from my own practice and perspective. What I describe is based on actual events and documented working processes. The Field Notes are not a scientific study; my observations and assessments have not been empirically tested.

To protect the people involved, I reconstructed images of student work from anonymized individual elements. Names and other identifying details were removed or altered.

Without me, this text would not be here. I am responsible for the decisions I make and for the traces the AI leaves in the text.
